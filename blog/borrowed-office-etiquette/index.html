Borrowing Someone's Office? 9 Etiquette Rules to Follow

October 9, 2026

When a colleague lends you their office for the day, they're trusting you with their space. Get it right and they'll keep offering it. Get it wrong once and the whole team hears about it.

Here are the rules that keep office sharing friendly.

1. Leave it exactly as you found it

Chair height, monitor position, the stack of papers on the corner of the desk: put everything back. A quick photo when you arrive makes this easy.

2. Don't open what's closed

Drawers, cabinets, bags and notebooks are off limits. If something confidential is lying out, leave it where it is.

3. Bring your own things

Use your own charger, headphones and mug. Borrowing the office holder's personal things, even small ones, is the fastest way to make them stop offering.

4. Take your rubbish with you

No cups, wrappers or half-finished coffee left behind. Wipe the desk if you ate at it.

5. Keep calls considerate

A private office is great for calls, but its walls may be thinner than you think. Keep the volume reasonable, especially if neighbors are used to a quiet room.

6. Read the office holder's note

Office holders often leave a note with their offer, like where the monitor cable is or which light switch works. Read it before you start moving things.

7. Speak up about anything broken

If something breaks or goes missing while you're there, tell the office holder the same day. A quick message is much better than them finding out.

8. Only book the days you'll use

Holding an office "just in case" keeps it from someone who really needs it. If your plans change, cancel straight away so it goes to the next person waiting.

9. Say thanks

A short thank-you message costs nothing and goes a long way. Office holders who feel appreciated keep sharing.

For office holders: make lending easy

  • Clear the desk of anything private before your away days.
  • Leave a short note about the setup: monitor, dock, Wi-Fi, lights.
  • If you come back early, cancel your offer as soon as you know and let your guest know.
