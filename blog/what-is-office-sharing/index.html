What Is Office Sharing? How Empty Offices Become Free Space

October 9, 2026

Walk through almost any building with private offices on a Friday and you'll see it: doors open, lights off, nobody inside. Meanwhile, someone who doesn't have an office is taking a call from a stairwell.

Office sharing is the simple fix. When someone is away, their office doesn't have to sit empty.

How office sharing works

  1. Office holders offer their office for the specific days they'll be away: on leave, traveling, working from home, in court, teaching.
  2. People without an office request one for the days they'll be in.
  3. Requests are matched to offers by day. If nothing is free, the request waits in line until something opens up.
  4. The office holder gets their office back automatically when the away days are over.

Nobody loses their office. It's only shared on the days its office holder picks.

Who it's for

Office sharing works best where some people have private offices and others don't. That includes:

  • Law and accounting firms, where partners are often out with clients or in court.
  • Universities, where faculty offices sit empty outside teaching hours and adjuncts share space.
  • Hospitals and clinics, where staff rotate between sites.
  • Agencies and consultancies, where people spend days on client sites.
  • Any hybrid team with more people than offices.

Why teams do it

  • More people get a quiet room. New hires, part-timers and visitors get somewhere to focus instead of a corner of the kitchen.
  • No new space needed. You use the offices you already pay for, more of the time.
  • Office holders keep what's theirs. Nothing changes on the days they're in.
  • Less back-and-forth. Fewer "is anyone using this room?" messages.

What makes it work

Sharing only works if it's effortless and fair. Offering an office should take seconds. Matching should happen without someone playing coordinator. And there should be clear, shared expectations about how to treat a borrowed office. We cover those in our etiquette guide and our Community Guidelines.
