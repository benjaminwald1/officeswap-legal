Hot Desking vs. Office Sharing: What's the Difference?

October 9, 2026

As offices adjust to hybrid schedules, two ideas come up again and again: hot desking and office sharing. They sound alike, but they work very differently, and they suit different teams.

What is hot desking?

With hot desking, nobody has an assigned desk. Everyone picks from a shared pool each day, either first come, first served or through a booking system. Desks are cleared at the end of the day so the next person can use them.

Good for: open-plan floors, teams that are rarely in at the same time, and companies redesigning their space from scratch.

Watch out for: people losing a space of their own, setting up from scratch every morning, and the daily scramble when more people come in than expected.

What is office sharing?

With office sharing, people keep their own offices. On the days an office holder is away, they offer their office, and someone without one can use it for those days. When the office holder is in, the office is theirs as usual.

Good for: workplaces with private offices, like law firms, clinics, universities and agencies, where some people have an office and others don't, and where office holders are often out for travel, court, classes, client visits or leave.

Watch out for: it only works if office holders actually offer their away days, so offering has to take seconds.

Side by side

  • Assigned space: hot desking has none. With office sharing, office holders keep theirs.
  • What gets booked: hot desking books a desk from a pool. Office sharing books a specific office on the days its office holder offered.
  • Change needed: hot desking usually means a new floor plan and policy. Office sharing works with the offices you already have.
  • Who gives up what: with hot desking, everyone gives up a fixed spot. With office sharing, office holders give up nothing on the days they're in.
  • Best fit: hot desking suits open-plan floors. Office sharing suits buildings full of private offices.

Can you do both?

Yes. Many workplaces have a few unassigned guest desks alongside private offices. Treat the guest desks as always available and the private offices as available only on the days their office holders offer them, and you get the best of both.
